# Fable Gets Herk-Level Edits Without Chrome

Fable can reach most of what the Herk Edit Kit and top short-form editors do, on this laptop, for free and without sending footage anywhere, because almost everything that makes those edits good is measurable with FFmpeg and expressible as numbers plus rules, not taste that only a human or a cloud service has. The two parts Fable cannot match today are speed and inspection: HyperFrames renders at roughly **3 fps on this Ryzen 3 / 7.3 GB machine** (7 to 8 minutes for a 44-second draft) and eats gigabytes of temp disk per minute, while the Herk kit's real edge is a verification discipline (three truthful openings, frame-by-frame reading of the first 2.5 seconds, re-measuring the render) that Fable does not perform at all. The fix is to invert the pipeline: measure the peg and the footage with FFmpeg (every pass takes about 2 seconds for a 30-second reel), keep a single `plan.json` as the source of truth, render captions and mixes through libass and FFmpeg (**4 to 13 seconds for 30 seconds of 1080x1920**, measured here), use HyperFrames only for the few seconds that need GSAP motion, and spend Claude Opus 5.5 tokens on judgment calls over contact sheets rather than on watching video. At list prices that costs about **$0.90 to $1.50 per reel** on Opus 5.5 (zero within a Pro/Max plan), and the stealth requirement is satisfiable: HyperFrames telemetry, update checks, Google Fonts, Groq and stock B-roll are each a documented switch, leaving zero outbound calls when transcription is local. The build plan at the end puts a peg analyzer, a hook gate and an FFmpeg caption/mix path first, moves HyperFrames to an opt-in graphics layer, and skips persona panels, Remotion, madmom and automated stock audio.

## Every agent editor converged on the same design, and Fable already has its skeleton

The open-source field has settled on one architecture: a coding agent (Claude Code or Codex) reads a word-timed transcript, writes a declarative timeline (HyperFrames HTML/GSAP or Remotion TSX) or drives FFmpeg directly, renders locally, and runs lint-style validators. The Herk Edit Kit is "Nate Herk's video-editing kit for Codex and Claude Code, built on HyperFrames," with 15 skills, 406 draft motion cards, and validators for caption timing, source mapping, scene coverage and beat sync; it publishes no model recommendation, no frame-review mechanism and no metrics, and tells users it "is an agent-guided workflow; review the actual motion and audio before publishing" ([herk-edit-kit](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit)). free247editor, which Fable grew from, wraps that kit behind an inbox/outbox loop and a `my-rules.md` memory and warns that "Editing uses a lot of usage" ([riecodes/free247editor](https://github.com/riecodes/free247editor)). kevinbadi's open-edits adds a `reverse-engineer` loop that measures a reference, scaffolds a `<style>-edit` skill and re-measures a demo, rejecting it when "Any metric off by more than about 25%" ([open-edits SKILL.md](https://github.com/kevinbadi/open-edits/blob/main/skills/reverse-engineer/SKILL.md)); video-style-kit does the same with a `recipe.json` plus `STYLE.md` pair and a `calibrate` step that draws its captions over a reference frame, but it is Apple-Silicon-only ([video-style-kit](https://github.com/baneets/video-style-kit)). Only one project, krusemediallc/video-editor-agent, documents the agent looking at rendered frames with vision at all ([video-editor-agent](https://github.com/krusemediallc/video-editor-agent)). No project in this set publishes a token count, a dollar cost, a quality metric or the Claude model it ran on, and every one keeps a human for transcript proofing, cut approval and the final viewing ([aident.ai HyperFrames guide](https://aident.ai/blog/edit-videos-claude-code-hyperframes); [LearnWithHasan](https://learnwithhasan.com/guide/claude-code-video-editing/)).

That means the differentiators are no longer the engine but three things: whether the agent inspects its own output, how style is captured and remembered, and how feedback flows back. Fable's current `fable.mjs` already covers the skeleton well: silence-and-phrase cuts, word captions with a 2-frame lead, a hook card, a `sidechaincompress` music duck, a two-stage `loudnorm` to -14 LUFS, lavfi-synthesized SFX, optional Pexels/Pixabay B-roll, an inbox watcher with a stop-growing check, and `edit.json` as an editable plan. What it lacks lines up exactly with the differentiators: no peg analysis, no hook selection logic (the card is simply the first seven words, so it will happily open on "So, uh, today I want to talk about"), no inspection of the rendered first frame, no re-measurement of the output, and a render path that costs minutes and gigabytes per draft. The rest of this report takes each of those in turn and ends with the order to build them in.

## Copying a peg means twelve numbers and one look, and FFmpeg gets the numbers in under 15 seconds

The playbooks that "copy a style" agree on what to measure. Nate Herk's playbook, built from "Nate's 27 top Instagram Reels (March to September 2026) frame by frame," records pacing (shots average **1.1 to 1.4 s**, none past ~2.8 s, first cut at **0.6 to 1.0 s** "on the first charged word," speech starting within 0.1 s, word gaps median 0.04 s at 4.0 to 4.7 words per second), layout shares (split 30 to 42%, full B-roll 50 to 55%, punch-in 5 to 15%, speaker on screen ~45% and never absent more than ~3.5 s), caption rules (1 to 3 words per chunk, 2 frames early, Montserrat ExtraBold all caps ~62 to 64 px, colour on 25 to 45% of chunks) and sound (bed ~11 dB under voice, master near -14 LUFS, true peak at or below -1.5 dBTP) ([EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/docs/EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md)). open-edits' `report.json` carries the same families as fields: shot median/p10/p90, cuts per minute, under-1s and over-3s share, face-on-screen percentage and position, palette, visual events per minute, LUFS, pauses over 0.35 s, words and wpm ([study.py](https://github.com/kevinbadi/open-edits/blob/main/skills/reverse-engineer/scripts/study.py)). The 2021 Google paper on editing transfer reduces per-shot style to framing, playback speed and lighting ([arXiv:2105.06988](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.06988)); the practitioner kits are richer than the academic one.

Almost all of this is cheap on this laptop. Measured during the research on a synthetic 30-second 1080x1920 clip with the installed FFmpeg 9.0.2: `scdet` ran 900 frames at 484 fps in 2.1 s; `select='gt(scene,0.3)',showinfo` took 2.1 s; a `signalstats` luminance pass at 2 fps took 2.0 s; `ebur128` took 0.27 s; a 6x5 contact sheet took 1.9 s. The entire FFmpeg battery on a 60 to 90-second reel therefore finishes in roughly 10 to 30 seconds with no Python installed. The thresholds to start from are practitioner settings rather than benchmarks: FFmpeg's own docs say `scdet` "Good values are in the [8.0, 14.0] range" ([ffmpeg-devel scdet patch](https://lists.ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-devel/2020-May/262639.html)), open-edits uses `select scene` at 0.22 with a 0.25 s merge and instructs "Don't trust a cut list you haven't checked" ([open-edits SKILL.md](https://github.com/kevinbadi/open-edits/blob/main/skills/reverse-engineer/SKILL.md)), and PySceneDetect's benchmark shows its AdaptiveDetector reaching F1 91.6 on BBC Planet Earth but only 55.8 on ClipShots, with every fast-cut detector missing fades ([scenedetect.com/benchmarks](https://www.scenedetect.com/benchmarks/)). Two reel-specific cautions follow: PySceneDetect's default `--min-scene-len 0.6s` silently merges the sub-second shots reels are made of, so use 0.2 to 0.3 s, and whip zooms and full-frame flashes will produce misses and false positives that only a contact-sheet check catches.

The "one look" is the part FFmpeg cannot do: font family and weight, highlight colour, caption animation, and whether the peg's layout is split, full-bleed or picture-in-picture. video-style-kit measures text position and size with OCR-style boxes and then has Claude read contact sheets "to name the font and colours, then checks its numbers by drawing its captions over a reference frame" ([video-style-kit](https://github.com/baneets/video-style-kit)); open-edits makes the visual pass mandatory: "Don't build a skill from numbers alone. Step 2 is mandatory" ([open-edits SKILL.md](https://github.com/kevinbadi/open-edits/blob/main/skills/reverse-engineer/SKILL.md)). The constraint when handing that look to Claude is resolution: Claude "might hallucinate or make mistakes when interpreting low-quality, rotated, or very small images under 200 pixels," and its localization is approximate ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). A 180x320 tile on a 6x5 sheet is well under that floor for reading glyphs, so the right input is one sheet of caption-band crops at about 540x150 each for font and colour, one or two shot-midpoint sheets for layout and palette, and three to six full frames (hook frame, one per layout type). That whole peg look costs around 10 to 15k image tokens, a few cents on any current model.

The legal line is clear enough to encode as a rule. Pacing, layout geometry, caption chunking and sound levels are techniques, which the idea-expression and scènes à faire doctrines leave unprotected ([Lexplug: Data East v. Epyx](https://www.lexplug.com/casebrief/data_east_usa_inc_v_epyx_inc__68f945e5719bc08738bf89f2)); the peg's footage, music, script and caption wording are protected expression. All three kits encode this identically: Herk asks that the reference be a video "the student supplies and has permission to use" and that the agent "not copy the reference's script or assets" ([reference-analysis.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/.agents/skills/short-form-edit/references/reference-analysis.md)); open-edits says "Don't include reference assets: footage, music, logos, branding fonts or show names" ([open-edits SKILL.md](https://github.com/kevinbadi/open-edits/blob/main/skills/reverse-engineer/SKILL.md)). Fable should take a user-supplied file rather than a yt-dlp URL, store the peg as `peg-style.json` plus `peg-style.md`, and re-plan the user's own transcript against those numbers instead of copying the peg's cut times.

## The hook is a selection problem over the user's own words, gated by objective checks

Platforms publish directional guidance, not drop-off curves. TikTok says to "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds" and "Prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds," and recommends 5 to 10 words per second of on-screen text ([TikTok Ads Help](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices)); YouTube measures "Stayed to watch" as "The percentage of times viewers stayed to watch past the initial seconds of a Short" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12220281)), and on YouTube's own blog a top Shorts creator says "you have one second to hook someone" ([YouTube Official Blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shorts-deep-dive/)); Meta's Reels ads analysis found that creative with sound on, safe-zone framing and at least one extra element such as "a hook in the first few seconds" saw a **30% higher 3-second play rate** and 93% higher ThruPlay ([Adobe citing Meta](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2024/11/04/unlock-potential-reels-best-practices-for-creative-success-using-adobe-creative-cloud)). Every per-second percentage circulating online (71% decide in 3 s, 65% leave by 1.8 s) traces to unnamed "industry data" and should not be built into the product. One academic finding is useful as a design constraint: a model of "message sensation value" validated on 14,492 short videos found behavioral engagement follows an inverted U, so moderate intensity beats maximal stimulation ([arXiv 2604.19995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19995)), which agrees with Herk's "Do not make loudness the only hook."

What works, across the Herk kit, Opus Clip, vidIQ and Filipino sources, is a small catalog: result or proof first, problem or recognition first, a claim or command that names the thing, a single non-yes/no question, and a motion pattern interrupt that relates to the content. Herk's story template opens with "a claim or command that names the tool or person" ([EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/docs/EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md)); Opus Clip's four formulas put the stopping element inside 2 s, the value by 3 s and the proof within 10 to 15 s, and advise rotating formulas to avoid fatigue ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-hook-formulas)); vidIQ asks for three layers that agree, a 3 to 7-word text hook over a 5 to 10-word verbal one ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/)). A Filipino creator's version is to "cut your climax part of your content then put it on your 1-3sec video" ([TikTok @ishanglamona](https://www.tiktok.com/@ishanglamona/video/7469279556206071048)), and the PH-specific advice is Taglish captions, relatable "POV" scenarios and a hard hook in the first 1 to 2 seconds ([Altrue PH](https://altrue.ph/articles-and-news/tiktok-in-the-philippines-users-stats-what-time-to-post-2025-edition/)). The anti-patterns are equally consistent: "Hey guys" lead-ins, logo bumpers, a mandatory STOP stamp or 0.3-second flash on every video, "wait for it," and promises the footage cannot pay off; Herk's skill says to "Vary the device between reels; do not automatically stamp STOP on every video" and to "Reject vague teasing and any promise that this footage cannot fulfill" ([short-form-edit SKILL.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/.agents/skills/short-form-edit/SKILL.md)).

For an agent that owns only talking-head footage and a transcript, this turns hook writing into hook selection. The LLM should choose and compress a sentence that exists in the transcript, cite the word indices it compressed, keep the speaker's qualifiers and language mix (Taglish stays Taglish), name what is visible or spoken by 3 s, and point to the payoff timestamp; a candidate with no payoff is invalid. Herk's plan schema already stores an `opening` object with `benefit`, `evidenceBy3` and `payoffTime` and insists "Do not manufacture a claim for a hook. Keep the speech's qualifications" ([curiosity-and-entertainment.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/.agents/skills/short-form-edit/references/curiosity-and-entertainment.md)). A code-side check then enforces at least 80% content-token overlap between the on-screen text and its source span, and the renderer applies case.

Ranking three candidates without audience data is where Herk is most prescriptive and most honest. The skill says to "Build three materially different rough openings using the same truthful source speech," compare "the first three seconds for topic clarity, curiosity, muted comprehension, and follow-through," choose and record the reason, inspect the encoded opening "frame by frame, including its first frame," log it as a distinct "opening-hook gate," and "never call internal hook comparisons an audience A/B test" ([short-form-edit SKILL.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/.agents/skills/short-form-edit/SKILL.md)). Its quality gates fail a "Caption-only opening," a "long audio lead-in," a "masked consonant," and require an "Encoded first frame, immediately meaningful image" ([quality-gates.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/.agents/skills/short-form-edit/references/quality-gates.md)). Most of these gates are objective and free: speech onset at or before 0.15 s, hook text present on frame 0, text box outside the mouth band, no near-black frame in the first 3 s, caption lead of 2 frames, the first word's level within ~6 dB of any SFX peak in the same window, and a payoff time that exists. Only the muted-comprehension test needs Claude, and it needs just 13 frames at 540x960 of a 3.5-second animatic with the question "What is this video about and why keep watching?" with no transcript attached. The sound-off rules follow from the same sources: captions 60 to 75 px at 1080x1920 with stroke or shadow, inside a safe band roughly 500 to 1400 px from the bottom ([ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/instagram-reels-captions.html)), with the top 14%, bottom 35% and 6% sides clear ([House of Marketers safe zones](https://houseofmarketers.com/guide-to-safe-zones-tiktok-facebook-instagram-stories-reels/)), and nothing covering the mouth "least of all in the first second" ([EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/docs/EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md)). Fable's current behaviour of suppressing captions under the hook card works against the muted viewer; the card should sit above the caption band or the captions should keep running.

## Sound design is a palette table and one filtergraph; the hard part is where the music comes from

Herk's playbook is the only written source with concrete SFX rules, and they are easy to encode: "Sound effects follow visible actions, not every cut: a soft air whoosh on a real spatial move, a damped tap when a logo lands, a dry click for small UI, paper for documents, one short warm tone for a check, a dry stop for an X," with "laser zaps, pitch-drop plops, sci-fi chirps, and a whoosh on every cut" banned and repeated items varied "so a list doesn't sound like a percussion loop" ([EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/docs/EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md)). Independent editors converge on a density ceiling of 3 to 5 sound moments per short, whooshes under 0.3 s and well below dialogue, and the rule that "if the viewer notices the whoosh itself, the effect has stopped working" ([NemoVideo](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/ai-sound-effect-generator-short-videos); [Sonilo](https://sonilo.com/blog/guides/transition-effect-sound-video-edits)). Comedy adds that the sting lands "a beat after the line ends, never on the last word" and that silence after a punchline is itself the effect ([Morphic](https://morphic.com/resources/sounds/rimshot-sound-effect); [Finchley](https://finchley.co.uk/finchley-learning/best-practices-for-comedy-video-editing-on-youtube)). Fable's current cue logic (a tap at 0.1 s, a pop when the hook has two lines, a click on every cut that removed a second or more) is the "whoosh on every cut" pattern in miniature; cues should instead be bound to visual events from the plan (card lands, B-roll enters, check or X) with a per-30-second cap and a no-cue zone around the first spoken word.

Timing precision matters more than beat tracking for talking-head content. Herk targets "within 2 frames of word onset for captions and impact events" and aligns "the effect's audible impact with the visual landing within two frames," and never moves speech to a grid: "Cut between phrases, never inside one," "Never speed the voice up," "Cut only after a word fully decays; transcripts end words 40 to 80 ms before the consonant dies" ([short-form-edit SKILL.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/.agents/skills/short-form-edit/SKILL.md); [EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/docs/EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md)). Perception research backs a one-frame target: lip-sync detectability begins around +45/-125 ms and jitter between successive events is noticed at about 40 ms ([ITU-R BT.1359 summary](https://www.forasoft.com/learn/audio-for-video/articles-audio/lip-sync-itu-r-bt-1359-tolerance-windows); [SPIE 3959](https://medicalimaging.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/3959/0000/Sensitivity-to-auditory-visual-asynchrony-and-to-jitter-in-auditory/10.1117/12.387160.full)), so the policy is to align the SFX peak sample (not the file start) to the visual landing within ±33 ms and keep the offset constant. When a beat grid is wanted, librosa's `beat_track` (Ellis 2007 dynamic programming) is the default and was "best at estimating average global tempo" in a 2021 comparison, while aubio runs in ~0.1 s per 30-second excerpt at a lower F-measure of 57 versus 74 to 76 for neural trackers, and madmom is unmaintained against current NumPy ([librosa beat.py](https://github.com/librosa/librosa/blob/main/librosa/beat.py); [arXiv 2401.00209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.00209); [arXiv 2011.02619](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.02619)). The useful move is Herk's: shift the music bed within one bar so a downbeat lands on the second spoken line, and snap only B-roll-only cuts to beats ([EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/docs/EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md)).

Mixing and mastering need nothing Fable does not already ship. The local FFmpeg 9.0.2 build exposes `sidechaincompress`, two-pass `loudnorm`, `ebur128`, `afftdn`, `arnndn` (native in this build; it needs only a public-domain `.rnnn` model from GregorR's set), `deesser`, `speechnorm` and `alimiter` ([FFmpeg filters](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html); [GregorR/rnnoise-models](https://github.com/GregorR/rnnoise-models)). Herk's `mix.mjs` is the reference recipe: sum stems with `amix=normalize=0`, measure with `loudnorm=I=-14:TP=-1.2:LRA=11:print_format=json`, apply the measured values with `linear=true`, follow with `alimiter`, and remux the master over the render because "HyperFrames re-encodes audio slightly quieter" ([mix.mjs](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/.agents/skills/motion-showreel/scripts/mix.mjs)). Fable's single-pass `loudnorm` should become that two-pass form, every stem should be forced to stereo before measuring (Herk: "A mono file reads 3 dB lower and one reel shipped too loud because of it"), and the bed-to-voice ratio should be verified after normalization rather than assumed from the `volume=0.2` constant ([EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md](https://github.com/nateherkai/herk-edit-kit/blob/main/docs/EDITING-PLAYBOOK.md)). Platform loudness is undocumented by TikTok and Meta; -14 LUFS integrated at -1.5 dBTP, measured on the final encoded stereo file, satisfies the consensus and is not punished by the downward-only normalization vendors report ([OpenClip](https://openclip.app/learn/audio-normalization)). Mood-to-tempo has no published formula; the defensible approach is a vote between library tags, measured tempo and RMS, and a transcript tone label mapped to a BPM band (Herk: "a darker, confident beat for claims and news, lo-fi or chill groove for methods").

The constraint that actually shapes the build is licensing, and it rules out automation for music. Pixabay's license allows use "without having to attribute the author" in commercial video, but its public API covers only images and videos and its terms say "Data mining, extraction, scraping and the use of programs or robots for automatic data collection" is prohibited and "Bulk, large-scale or systematic copying of Content is strictly prohibited" ([Pixabay License Summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/); [Pixabay Terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/); [Pixabay API docs](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/)). YouTube's Audio Library guarantees are all phrased for YouTube and say nothing about other platforms ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882)). Jamendo has a real API with `speed` and mood tags, but every track requires attribution and many are non-commercial ([Jamendo /tracks](https://developer.jamendo.com/v3.0/tracks)). The one source that combines a free API with a clean license is Freesound, filtered to `license:"Creative Commons 0"` with `single_event:true` and a duration range, fetched as `preview-hq-mp3` without OAuth at 60 requests per minute ([Freesound API](https://freesound.org/docs/api/resources_apiv2.html); [Freesound API overview](https://freesound.org/docs/api/overview.html)), though its terms say commercial API use is "negotiated on a case by case basis" ([Freesound API Terms](https://freesound.org/help/tos_api/)). So Fable's `my-sounds/` folder design is right: the user downloads music by hand from Pixabay or Mixkit (no-credit, commercial, social video allowed) and Fable stores a sidecar JSON with source and license; SFX come from a one-time CC0 Freesound pack or the lavfi fallbacks, and the stealth profile makes zero audio calls per edit.

## On this laptop the render path, not the model, sets the ceiling

The performance picture is stark and well measured. HyperFrames auto-enters low-memory safe mode when total RAM is 8 GB or less, which "pins to 1 worker, uses screenshot capture, and skips auto-worker calibration," and its ~2x faster `drawElementImage` capture is on by default only on "macOS + hardware-GPU browser" (both from the installed CLI's `render --help`). The only published Windows numbers are from an RTX 4070 SUPER desktop: about 8.5 fps at one worker and 16 fps at three, and the GPU browser path was 3.2x faster than SwiftShader over a full render ([HyperFrames issue #5548](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/issues/5548)). The user's own 44-second draft in 7 to 8 minutes works out to 2.8 to 3.1 fps end to end on this Ryzen 3 iGPU, and `hyperframes doctor` reported 7.3 GB total RAM with 1.1 GB available and 3.4 GB of free disk. Disk is the more immediate failure mode: drafts "are captured at full composition size and scaled down afterward, so they cost as much time and disk as the final render," and one reporter needed about 96 GB for a 6.5-minute 1080p composition ([HyperFrames issue #5544](https://github.com/heygen-com/hyperframes/issues/5544)). Reaching a 30-second render in under two minutes needs 7.5 fps sustained; nothing in the HyperFrames knobs (`--no-low-memory-mode --workers 2`, `--browser-gpu`, `--experimental-fast-capture`, upgrading from 0.7.109 to 0.8.x for the Windows worker fixes) plausibly closes a 2.5x gap on this hardware, though each is worth one timed try.

The FFmpeg path clears the bar by an order of magnitude. Measured here on 30 seconds of 1080x1920 at 30 fps: per-word pop captions rendered through libass took 12.6 s with x264 veryfast, 6.0 s with the Radeon `h264_amf` encoder and 4.3 s with x264 ultrafast, against a 7.8 s no-caption baseline, and `\kf` karaoke in one event per phrase added only 0.4 s. ASS override tags cover the whole "HyperFrames look": per-word colour (`\c`), outline and shadow (`\bord`, `\shad`), scale pop (`\fscx`/`\fscy` with `\t`), position (`\an`, `\pos`), fades and blur, and karaoke timing (`\k`, `\kf`, `\ko`) ([Aegisub ASS tags](https://aegisub.org/docs/latest/ass_tags/)). What ASS cannot do is rounded CSS boxes, per-letter spring easing and GSAP timelines, which is exactly the residue HyperFrames should keep: render only the seconds that need motion graphics as a transparent WebM (`--format webm`) and composite them with `overlay` in the same FFmpeg pass as the captions and the mix. That hybrid also fixes disk, because the extract cache then holds a few seconds of frames rather than the whole reel; in the meantime `--frames-cache-dir off` (frames go to the render's workDir and are deleted when it ends) and a 540x960 proxy for analysis keep the current pipeline from failing on the 3.4 GB that is left.

Transcription is the other local cost, and the Taglish requirement decides the model. Whisper's own Fleurs table puts Tagalog WER at **tiny 65.6, base 45.8, small 27.7, medium 19.1, large-v2 13.8** ([Radford et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04356)), so anything below `small` is unusable and `medium` or `large-v3-turbo` is where Tagalog becomes clean; the `distil-*` models are English-only ([distil-large-v3](https://huggingface.co/distil-whisper/distil-large-v3)). faster-whisper `small` int8 transcribes 13 minutes in 1m42s using 1.5 GB on an i7-12700K ([faster-whisper README](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper)); on this slower CPU and with 1.1 GB free, the practical rule is `small` for drafts, `medium` or turbo for finals, and transcription never concurrent with a render. Groq's free Whisper tier (7,200 audio-seconds per hour, 28,800 per day) remains the fastest and most accurate option, and Groq says "By default, Groq does not retain customer data for inference requests" with a self-serve Zero Data Retention toggle that covers transcription ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits); [Groq data policy](https://console.groq.com/docs/your-data)), but the audio still leaves the machine, so stealth mode must either skip it or treat it as the one declared outbound call.

Stealth itself is a checklist, not a research problem. HyperFrames sends anonymous PostHog events to `us.i.posthog.com` for 17 commands, including fingerprints for sandbox and agent runtime; it is disabled by `HYPERFRAMES_NO_TELEMETRY=1` or `DO_NOT_TRACK=1`, confirmed locally by `hyperframes telemetry status` reporting "disabled." The other outbound endpoints in the CLI bundle are the npm update check (`HYPERFRAMES_NO_UPDATE_CHECK=1`), the feedback prompt (`HYPERFRAMES_NO_FEEDBACK=1`), auto-install and skills (`HYPERFRAMES_NO_AUTO_INSTALL=1`, `HYPERFRAMES_SKIP_SKILLS=1`), Google Fonts from inside compositions (vendor the fonts and set `HYPERFRAMES_FONT_CACHE_DIR`), and HeyGen OAuth (never call `login` or `publish`). Fable's own optional calls are Groq, Pexels and Pixabay, each already gated by an environment variable; Pexels allows 200 requests per hour and Pixabay requires 24-hour caching and forbids systematic downloads ([Pexels API](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/900005852323); [Pixabay API docs](https://pixabay.com/api/docs/)). On the Claude side, image uploads "are ephemeral and not stored beyond the duration of the API request" and are not used for training ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)), and the design below keeps the user's footage from ever needing to be sent: only contact sheets of the user's own render and the peg go to the model, and even those are optional. The one thing no document confirms is that `chrome-headless-shell` makes no connections of its own, so the watcher should be run once under a Windows Firewall outbound block or a packet capture before the stealth claim goes in the README.

## Claude should judge contact sheets at about a dollar a reel, not watch video

The model choice is settled by Anthropic's own guidance and prices. As of October 2026, Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`, released 22 September 2026) costs **$4 input / $20 output per million tokens** with cache reads at $0.20 (5% of input), 1M context and 128K output, and Anthropic says "start with Claude Opus 5.5 for most workloads" at its default `medium` effort, reserving Fable 5.1 ($10/$50) for cases where Opus at higher effort still falls short ([Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview.md); [Pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing.md)). Measured on agentic coding, Opus 5.5 at `low` solved about the same share of tasks as Sonnet 5.5 at its default for about 7% more per solved task, and `low` costs roughly half of `medium` per task, so varying effort per step on one model (low for triage and lint fixes, medium for the edit plan) beats a two-model setup and keeps one cache namespace ([Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence.md)). Anthropic's measured delegation economics say the same thing from the other side: "Delegation saved money in only two situations," routine work with a long cost tail and work larger than one context window; a 20 to 40-frame review fits in one context ([Optimizing for cost and intelligence](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/optimizing-for-cost-and-intelligence.md)). What Opus 5.5 brings that older models did not is sustained unattended work (an 18-hour run reported by a customer, output tokens more than 30% faster than Opus 5, fewer steps per task) and sharper positional reading of visuals, "where meaning depends on position rather than text" ([Opus 5.5 announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5); [Prompting Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)). Anthropic makes no claim about creative or aesthetic judgment, and concedes that for design work the model "falls back on a few default styles" unless told specific patterns to avoid, which is the strongest argument for putting the peg numbers and a "do not" list in the prompt rather than asking for taste ([Prompting Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)).

No Claude model accepts video or audio as input; the Files API maps only PDF, text and JPEG/PNG/GIF/WebP to content blocks, animated GIFs use only the first frame, and Opus 5.5 is "Text and images → text" ([Files API](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/files.md); [Opus 5.5 overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/opus-5-5/overview)). Images cost `⌈width/28⌉ × ⌈height/28⌉` tokens, and on the high-resolution tier a 1920x1080 frame is 2,691 tokens and is not downscaled, so a 3x3 contact sheet rendered at 1920x1080 costs the same 2,691 tokens as one raw frame, a 9x saving per frame while each cell stays at 640x360, above the 200 px reliability floor ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision.md)). Above 20 images in a request every image must be at most 2000 px on its long edge, so frames should be pre-resized to 1280 px or less and labelled with timestamps in text before each image. Motion is the honest limit: Anthropic documents still-image understanding and says counting and localization are approximate; it makes no claim that Claude infers cut rhythm or motion smoothness from frame sequences. Timing judgments therefore come from word timestamps, scene detection and audio levels, and frames are used to verify composition, caption placement, legibility and the first frame.

The worked cost model from the notes, using the cited prices and a ~25K-token stable prefix, gives one reel edit with a 300-word transcript, 10 tool rounds, 30 frames and two render passes at about **$1.50 on Opus 5.5 with raw 1080p frames, $1.08 with four contact sheets, and $0.90 with frame review delegated to Haiku 5.5**, with output and thinking tokens (~$0.60) the largest line; the same edit is roughly 0.5x on Sonnet 5.5 and 2.5x on Fable 5.1, and a follow-up round ("faster, bigger captions") adds $0.30 to $0.50 because the history is a cache read. This is a model, not a measurement; no project anywhere publishes real token figures for an agent edit, so `/usage` after two or three real runs is the calibration step. Under Pro or Max the dollar cost is zero within the plan; Anthropic publishes no tokens-per-window figure, only that Max 5x and 20x give 5x and 20x Pro's per-5-hour usage and that Claude Code weekly limits are 25% higher than before the 2026 promotion ([claude.com/pricing](https://claude.com/pricing); [support.claude.com](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/15910845)).

How Claude Code runs the loop matters for cost as much as which model. Skills load only their description (1,536 characters) until invoked and are re-attached after compaction keeping the first 5,000 tokens each, so the Fable SKILL.md should stay short with tables in `references/`; a `model:` field in skill frontmatter is a cache miss for that turn, while changing `effort` keeps the cache ([Claude Code skills](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills); [Claude Code prompt caching](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)). Hooks are a cost tool, "reducing context from tens of thousands of tokens to hundreds" by pre-filtering, and the `FileChanged` hook watches literal filenames, not directories ([Hooks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks); [Manage costs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs)). For unattended runs, `claude -p` with `--bare --permission-mode auto --permission-prompts none --max-budget-usd N --output-format json` gives a hard spend cap and `total_cost_usd` per edit, but bare mode "doesn't use your subscription login," so on a subscription the cheap pattern is Fable's existing Node watcher doing the deterministic work and invoking Claude only for the three judgment calls (hook selection, peg look, render review), or a Desktop scheduled task that polls `inbox/` and exits when empty ([Run Claude Code programmatically](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/headless); [Scheduled tasks](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/scheduled-tasks)). Anthropic also warns that Opus 5.5 on long tasks "keeps the user updated as it works, and some of those updates end the turn with text rather than a tool call," so an autopilot needs a checklist file and a bounded re-prompt rather than treating the first text turn as done ([Prompting Opus 5.5](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/prompting-claude-opus-5-5)). A standalone Fable app on the Agent SDK would have to bill an API key, because "Anthropic does not allow third party developers to offer claude.ai login or rate limits for their products" ([Agent SDK overview](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/agent-sdk/overview)).

## The build plan: measure first, render with FFmpeg, let Claude judge three things

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing from this research is that "letting Claude edit" is the wrong unit. The Herk kit's quality does not come from the model having taste; it comes from a measured style, a short list of truthful openings, event-bound sound, and a verification pass that reads the first frames and re-measures the file. Each of those is a number or a check that FFmpeg produces in seconds on a 15-watt laptop, and the model's job collapses to three judgment calls that fit in a few thousand image tokens. Once Fable is built that way, the user's footage never has to leave the machine, the peg never has to leave the machine, and the only question Claude is asked about pixels is "does this look like what the numbers say it should?"

The second shift is that HyperFrames should be demoted from engine to effect. On this hardware the browser render is the one component that cannot be made fast, light or disk-safe, and the captions and mixes that make up most of a talking-head reel do not need it. Keeping it for the few seconds of real motion graphics preserves the kit's best asset, its 406 motion cards and GSAP vocabulary, while removing the seven-minute draft and the multi-gigabyte temp directory. The open uncertainties worth testing early are empirical and local: whether `--no-low-memory-mode` with two workers and the GPU browser flag buys enough to keep HyperFrames in the draft loop at all, what faster-whisper `medium` actually costs in seconds per minute on this CPU, and what a real edit consumes in tokens under the Pro plan. Each is an afternoon's measurement, and each decides a line in the table above.
